# What you learned, and what is taught now

You took all three OAC mathematics courses: Algebra and Geometry, Finite Mathematics, and Calculus. OAC was the Grade 13 year, phased out in 2003, so you finished under a system that no longer exists.

Some of your non-recognition is linguistic and is dealt with in `reading-the-curriculum.md`. The rest is real. Content moved, and a great deal of it was cut.

**Caveat worth stating plainly.** The OAC course contents below are my reconstruction, and I could not reach any source to check them from this machine. You sat the courses, so where this conflicts with your memory, trust your memory and tell me, and I will correct the file.

## OAC Calculus → MCV4U strands A and B

**Still taught.** Derivatives. The power, sum, difference, product and chain rules. Curve sketching with the first and second derivative. Optimization. Derivatives of sine, cosine and exponential functions. Rate of change as the organizing idea.

**Cut entirely.**

- Formal limits and continuity. No epsilon-delta, no limit laws, no theorems. MCV4U A1.4 is explicitly informal and asks only that students recognize limits graphically and numerically.
- The quotient rule. Not an expectation. Rational functions are rewritten as products with negative exponents.
- Implicit differentiation.
- Related rates.
- Antiderivatives and integration.

**Emphasized now in a way it was not then.** Sketching the derivative from the graph of a function, with no equation anywhere. Reading the sign of f′ and f″ off a picture. This is a large part of strand B and it was a minor concern in your day, where the work was algebraic.

## OAC Algebra and Geometry → MCV4U strand C

This is the good news, and it changes your preparation plan.

**Still taught.** Geometric and algebraic vectors. Dot product. Cross product. Lines in two and three space in vector, parametric and scalar form. Planes and normals. Intersections of lines and planes. Distances. Systems of three linear equations and their geometric meaning.

**Cut entirely.** Conics and loci. Matrices and determinants. Formal geometric proof.

So MCV4U strand C is close to a subset of a course you have already taken. Not new material. Recall, twenty-five years cold. That is a different and much smaller job than learning it, and it means the ten hours I budgeted for vectors is probably four.

## OAC Finite Mathematics → mostly nowhere in this stream

The heaviest losses are here.

**Relocated.** Sequences and series moved down to Grade 11, and are now MCR3U strand C alongside compound interest and annuities. Counting and probability moved sideways into MDM4U, Data Management, a different Grade 12 course that a student takes instead of, or as well as, Calculus and Vectors.

**Cut from the university mathematics stream.** Matrices and matrix operations. Linear programming. Mathematical induction. Most of combinatorics beyond the binomial theorem, which survives only as MCR3U C1.6 via Pascal's triangle.

If you remember Finite Mathematics as a real course with real content, that content is largely gone. Nothing in MCV4U touches it.

## What is taught now that you probably did not do

- **Functions as an explicit subject.** Domain, range, inverse functions and the vertical line test are now named topics with their own expectations. You almost certainly used all of it without it being a unit.
- **Transformations as a unified treatment.** Everything is y = af(k(x − d)) + c, applied to every function family in turn. That single parameterization recurs in all three courses.
- **Financial mathematics.** Compound interest, annuities, present and future value, time-value-of-money solvers. MCR3U strand C section 3.
- **Technology throughout.** Graphing calculators, dynamic geometry software and spreadsheets appear in dozens of expectations.
- **Polynomial and rational inequalities as a formal topic.** MHF4U C4. This is the hidden prerequisite for curve sketching.

## What this means for your plan

Your calculus is intact and over-supplied. Your vectors are a refresh, not a build. Your Finite Mathematics is irrelevant to this course.

The genuinely new material for you is small: the functions-and-transformations framing, financial mathematics, and the graphical style of the derivative work. None of it is hard. All of it is unfamiliar in presentation rather than in substance.

Revised estimate for getting ready to tutor MCV4U: about six hours, not twelve. Four on vectors, two on the graphical derivative work in strand B.
