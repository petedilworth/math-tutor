# First-session diagnostic

Fourteen questions, about forty minutes. No calculator except where stated. Every question targets a skill the prerequisite map identifies as load-bearing for MCV4U.

The point is not to score him. It is to find which of six specific skills is broken, because each one breaks calculus in a different, predictable way. Give him the questions, watch him work, and say nothing.

Tell him plainly that it is not a test, it does not count, and you need to see what he can do unaided so you do not waste his time teaching things he already knows. Most students relax when told this and you get a truer picture.

---

## Section 1. Rational and polynomial algebra

*This section matters more than the rest combined. MCR3U A3.1, A3.3.*

**1.** Simplify and state all restrictions on x.

    (x² − 9) / (x² + x − 12)

**2.** Write as a single simplified fraction, and state restrictions.

    3/(x + 2) − 2/x

**3.** For f(x) = x² − 3x, simplify the expression [f(2 + h) − f(2)] / h as far as possible, then state what it approaches as h gets close to 0.

**4.** For f(x) = 1/x, simplify [f(a + h) − f(a)] / h as far as possible.

---

## Section 2. Exponents and radicals

*MCR3U B1.2, B1.3. Needed the moment the power rule meets a root.*

**5.** Evaluate without a calculator: 27^(2/3) and 16^(−3/4).

**6.** Write √(x³) · √x as a single power of x.

---

## Section 3. Function notation and domain

*MCR3U A1.2, A1.3.*

**7.** For f(x) = 2x² − 5, find and simplify f(a + 1) − f(a).

**8.** State the domain and range of f(x) = √(x − 4) + 2.

---

## Section 4. Factoring and inequalities

*MHF4U C3.2, C4.3. This is where every sign chart in curve sketching comes from.*

**9.** Factor fully: x³ − 4x² + x + 6

**10.** Using your answer to question 9, solve x³ − 4x² + x + 6 > 0.

---

## Section 5. Radians and logarithms

*MHF4U B1.1, B1.4, A1.4. Needed for the derivatives of sin, cos and exponentials.*

**11.** Convert 5π/6 radians to degrees, and give the exact value of cos(5π/6).

**12.** Solve for x: log₂ x + log₂(x − 2) = 3

---

## Section 6. Rate of change

*MHF4U D1.4, D1.7, D1.8. The direct on-ramp to MCV4U strand A.*

**13.** Sketch a graph of f′(x) below this description of f: the curve falls to a minimum at x = −1, rises to a maximum at x = 2, then falls away.

**14.** For f(x) = 3x², find the average rate of change from x = 1 to x = 4. Then describe, in words, how you would estimate the instantaneous rate of change at x = 1.

---

# Answer key

1. (x + 3)/(x + 4), with x ≠ 3 and x ≠ −4
2. (x − 4) / (x(x + 2)), with x ≠ 0 and x ≠ −2
3. h + 1, approaching 1
4. −1 / (a(a + h))
5. 9 and 1/8
6. x²
7. 4a + 2
8. Domain x ≥ 4, range y ≥ 2
9. (x + 1)(x − 2)(x − 3)
10. −1 < x < 2 or x > 3
11. 150°, and −√3/2
12. x = 4 only. The value −2 satisfies the quadratic but is outside the domain of the logarithm.
13. Negative before x = −1, zero at x = −1, positive between −1 and 2, zero at x = 2, negative after x = 2
14. Average rate of change is 15. To estimate the instantaneous rate at x = 1, compute the average rate over shrinking intervals starting at 1, such as 1 to 1.1, then 1 to 1.01, and watch the values settle. They approach 6.

---

# What each failure tells you

**Question 1, missing the restriction x ≠ 3.** Very common and not trivial. He is cancelling without tracking what the original expression forbade. In MCV4U this shows up as sloppy handling of the difference quotient and of rational function domains. Cheap to fix, worth fixing early.

**Question 2 wrong.** Unlike denominators are broken. This is the single highest-priority repair. He cannot do MCV4U A1.5 or A1.6 until it is fixed, and no amount of explaining limits will help.

**Question 3 wrong but question 1 and 2 right.** He can do the algebra but does not know what the expression is for. This is a concept gap, not a skill gap, and it is the easier of the two problems. Teach the secant-to-tangent picture.

**Question 4 wrong when question 3 was right.** The most informative single result in the diagnostic. It means he can do the difference quotient when it is polynomial expansion and cannot do it when it requires combining fractions. That is exactly the boundary where MCV4U students start failing. Repair MCR3U A3.3.

**Question 5 or 6 wrong.** Rational exponents are shaky. He will handle the power rule for x³ and fail on √x, which MCV4U A3.4 requires explicitly.

**Question 7 wrong.** Function notation is not secure. Rare in a student who passed MCR3U, but if it is broken, everything downstream is guesswork and this becomes the first priority instead.

**Question 9 wrong.** He cannot factor beyond quadratics, so he cannot use the factor theorem. Curve sketching and optimization both stall here.

**Question 10 wrong when 9 was right.** He can factor but cannot reason about sign across intervals. This is the specific gap that makes curve sketching feel impossible later, and almost no student recognizes it as the cause. Watch for whether he tries to solve it like an equation.

**Question 11 wrong.** Radians are not real to him yet. MCV4U A2.4, the derivative of sine, is unreachable until they are. Note that MCR3U works in degrees throughout and MHF4U introduces radians, so this gap points at Advanced Functions, not Grade 11.

**Question 12, giving both 4 and −2.** He applied the log laws correctly and did not check the domain. Minor, but it is the same habit as missing restrictions in question 1, and two instances make a pattern worth naming to him.

**Question 13 wrong.** The connection between the shape of f and the sign of f′ is missing. This is the core idea of MCV4U strand B and he will need it in week one.

**Question 14, second part vague.** He knows how to compute an average rate but not what it has to do with an instantaneous one. Same repair as question 3.

# Also worth recording

Things the answers will not tell you, which you should note during the session.

- Does he write anything when stuck, or does he stop?
- Does he check an answer without being asked?
- Does he reach for the calculator on question 5?
- Which questions did he skip, and did he come back?
- What does he say when you ask which one felt worst?

A student who gets nine of fourteen but attacks every question is in better shape than one who gets eleven and abandons anything unfamiliar. The second pattern is the one that breaks in MCV4U, because most of the course is unfamiliar.
